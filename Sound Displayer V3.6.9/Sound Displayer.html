Turns whatever is playing into geometry. Choose what to listen to.

For desktop audio, your browser asks what to share. Choose Entire screen and switch on system audio. Only the sound is read, and nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

What am I seeing?

Every shape is computed from the live sound. Nothing is pre-drawn or random decoration.

The 3, 6, 9 lamps

Each burst of sound has a life. 3 lights when it starts, 6 when it reaches its peak, and 9 when it ends. The same three colours mark those moments in every scene.

Vortex

Nine points on a circle, one for each octave of hearing, from deep bass at 1 to the highest treble at 9. A point swells when its octave is loud.

The six-sided path 1, 2, 4, 8, 7, 5 is what you get by doubling a number and adding up its digits, over and over. It never lands on 3, 6 or 9. Doubling a frequency is exactly one octave, so each spark that runs the path is the loudest tone climbing octave by octave. A tone whose digits add up to 3 or 6 bounces between those two, and a 9 stays at 9.

The shape in the middle is the raw sound wave, wrapped around a circle with your chosen symmetry.

Cymatics

Sand on a vibrating drum skin. The sand is shaken off the parts that move and collects on the still lines, the way real sand does on a real plate. Higher tones make more rings, and the symmetry sets how many spokes the pattern has. The sand only moves while there is sound.

Rosette

The wave is drawn against a slightly delayed copy of itself. A pure tone becomes a perfect circle, and richer sounds knot into loops. Copies are placed around the centre, so a steady tone at 6 draws the old “seed of life” figure.

Behind it are 369 points on a circle, each joined to its multiple. The pitch sets the multiplier, so the number of cusps grows as the tone rises.

Auto symmetry

Auto steps through 3, 6 and 9, holding each for about 15 seconds. It waits for the next beat to make the change, so the switch lands on a start. Choosing a number yourself turns Auto off.

About 3, 6, 9

The saying about 3, 6 and 9 is widely credited to Nikola Tesla, though it has not been found in his own writing. The number patterns used here are real arithmetic either way. One nice fact: the ratio 3 : 6 : 9 is 1 : 2 : 3, the first three harmonics of any musical note.
